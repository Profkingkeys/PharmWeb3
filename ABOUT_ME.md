# About Me

## Kingsley Umoh — Profkingkeys

I am a **Founder, Full-Stack Developer, AI/Prompt Engineer and Pharmacy Student** building at the intersection of software, healthcare and emerging technology.

My main venture is **PharmWeb3**, a healthcare technology project exploring how pharmacy practice can be combined with intelligent software, accessible digital services and carefully scoped Web3 infrastructure.

### How I think about engineering

I am interested in the entire path from an idea to a usable product:

**problem → product → interface → backend → AI → data → infrastructure → measurement**

That is why my repositories span healthcare interfaces, APIs, AI workflows, authentication, WebView/mobile integration, databases, telemetry, Web3 metadata and product prototypes.

### AI

AI is becoming a normal part of software engineering, so I treat prompt engineering and agent design as engineering disciplines.

I work with:

- prompt design;
- tool calling;
- structured outputs;
- agent state;
- model routing and fallback;
- workflow automation;
- evaluation boundaries;
- research assistance.

The goal is not to add a chatbot to every product. The goal is to make AI useful where it actually reduces work or improves a workflow.

### Full-stack & SaaS

I build both user-facing interfaces and the systems behind them. My work includes Node/Express applications, FastAPI services, authentication, email flows, real-time communication, database-backed workflows and SaaS-style product architecture.

### Mobile

I also work across the web/mobile boundary using **Android, Kotlin, Java and WebView**. One of my projects demonstrates how web applications can keep a familiar request interface while delegating network operations through a native Android bridge.

### Pharmacy

I am a pharmacy student, which gives my healthcare work a different starting point: product decisions are informed by pharmacy practice, medication workflows and the reality that healthcare software has consequences beyond the screen.

### Long-term research direction

One long-term goal is to use AI to help researchers work through large biomedical evidence collections more efficiently.

That means building better tools for:

- literature discovery;
- evidence extraction;
- structured comparison;
- research knowledge bases;
- reproducible analysis;
- hypothesis generation for human researchers.

I do **not** present AI as a replacement for scientists, pharmacists, physicians or clinical research.
